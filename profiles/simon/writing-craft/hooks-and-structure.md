# Hooks and Structure (Julian Shapiro Framework)

How to write intros that hook readers and structures that keep them engaged. Adapted from Julian Shapiro's writing guide for social media posts.

---

## The Hook Principle

> "A captivating intro buys goodwill with readers so they overlook an imperfect middle."

**What this means for social posts:**
- First 1-2 lines determine if people keep reading
- A strong hook creates momentum
- Readers will forgive weaker middle content if you hook them hard

---

## What Is a Hook?

**A hook is a half-told story.**

You raise a question then tease only part of the answer:

### Hook Types

#### 1. Questions
Pose an intriguing question, but don't give the full answer.

**Example:**
```
Why do 90% of startups fail in the first year?

(Hint: it's not what you think)
```

#### 2. Narratives
Share the start of a narrative, but withhold the conclusion.

**Example:**
```
I just lost $50K in 30 days.

Here's what I learned:
```

#### 3. Research
Highlight research findings, but only a small portion.

**Example:**
```
I analyzed 500 viral posts.

They all do these 3 things:
```

#### 4. Arguments
Make an unexpected claim, but don't explain how it's true.

**Example:**
```
Your competitors' best move is your best opportunity.

Let me explain:
```

## Hook Formulas for Social Media

### Formula 1: Transformation Hook
```
[Time period] ago: [bad state]
Today: [good state]

What changed:
```

**Example:**
```
6 months ago: 200 followers, zero engagement
Today: 50K followers, inbound deals weekly

What changed:
```

### Formula 2: Pattern Recognition Hook
```
I [analyzed/studied/tested] [number] [things].

Here's what [successful ones] all do:
```

**Example:**
```
I analyzed 100 LinkedIn posts with 1M+ views.

Here's what they all do:
```

### Formula 3: Contrarian Hook
```
Everyone says [common belief].

They're wrong. Here's why:
```

**Example:**
```
Everyone says "post daily for growth."

They're wrong. Here's why:
```

### Formula 4: Stakes-First Hook
```
If you [common action], you're [negative outcome].

Here's the better way:
```

**Example:**
```
If you're optimizing for virality, you're leaving money on the table.

Here's the better way:
```

### Formula 5: Story Hook
```
[Dramatic opening line about situation]

[Context that raises stakes]

Here's what happened:
```

**Example:**
```
My co-founder quit 48 hours before our product launch.

We had $50K in pre-orders and no tech team.

Here's what happened:
```

## Two Purposes of Hooks

### 1. Compel readers to continue
- Switches on storytelling machinery in their heads
- Creates "I need to know what happens next" feeling

### 2. Identify what's interesting to write about
- If you can't hook people, the topic might not be interesting
- A good hook forces you to find novel angles

---

## Hook Psychology: The Information Gap

**Principle:** Humans are wired to close information gaps.

**How it works:**
1. Hook presents partial information
2. Brain wants to complete the picture
3. Reader keeps reading to close the gap

**Example:**
```
"I made one change and revenue doubled."
↓
What was the change? (Information gap)
↓
Reader keeps reading
```

## Structure: Building on Your Hook

Once you've hooked them, maintain engagement with structure:

### The 3-Part Structure for Social Posts

#### Part 1: Hook (Line 1-2)
- Creates "wait... what?" moment
- Raises question or creates tension

#### Part 2: Value Delivery (Middle)
- Answers the question posed by hook
- Provides framework, insight, or story
- Builds stakes as you go

#### Part 3: Payoff (End)
- Satisfying conclusion
- Clear takeaway or CTA
- Ends on implications, not hype

---

## Sustaining Engagement Through the Middle

### The Peak-End Rule

> "People judge an experience largely based on how they felt at its most intense point and at its end."

**Applied to social posts:**
- Have a captivating hook (buys goodwill)
- Have at least one peak of insight or surprise (the "aha!" moment)
- Have an ending that justifies why it was worth reading

**Implication:** Not every line has to be perfect. Focus on:
1. Strong hook
2. One peak moment
3. Satisfying ending

---

## Dopamine Hits: Keeping Readers Engaged

**Concept:** Sprinkle moments of insight throughout your post.

**What counts as a dopamine hit:**
- "Ahh, that's cool!"
- "That's fascinating!"
- "That's profound!"
- "I need to remember this!"

### How to Create Dopamine Hits

**1. Novelty**
- Counter-intuitive insights
- Counter-narrative takes
- Surprising data
- Elegant articulations

**2. Specificity**
- "3 tactics" beats "some tactics"
- "47% increase" beats "big increase"
- "In 90 days" beats "quickly"

**3. Universal Truths**
- Insights that make people say "yes, exactly!"
- Articulating what everyone thinks but no one says

## Examples: Hook → Structure → Payoff

### Example 1: LinkedIn Post

**Hook:**
```
Most founders optimize for raising money.

The best optimize for not needing it.
```

**Structure:**
```
Here's why:

1. When you need money, you negotiate from weakness
2. When you don't, you negotiate from strength
3. Optionality is your biggest asset

I've advised 50+ startups. The pattern is clear:

Companies that can say "no" to investors
get better terms when they say "yes."
```

**Payoff:**
```
Don't just build a business.
Build optionality.
```

---

### Example 2: Twitter Thread

**Hook (Tweet 1):**
```
I just spent $10K testing 12 LinkedIn strategies.

Only 3 worked.

Here's what I learned:

🧵
```

**Structure (Tweets 2-9):**
```
2/ Most advice says "post daily."

I tested posting 1x, 3x, 5x, 7x per week.

Result: 3x/week won by 2.4x engagement.

3/ Most advice says "use carousels."

I tested carousels, videos, images, text.

Result: Documents beat carousels by 1.3x.

[Continue pattern: claim → test → result]
```

**Payoff (Final tweet):**
```
10/ Bottom line:

Test > trust.

Your audience is different. Your timing is different. Your voice is different.

Copy the method, not the tactics.
```

## Advanced: Withholding Information

**Technique:** Decide which answers to reserve until the end.

**Why it works:** Creates sustained tension throughout the post.

**Example:**
```
Hook: "I made one change and revenue doubled."

Middle: Talks about context, tests, failures

End: Reveals the actual change
```

**Key:** The withheld information must be worth the wait.

## Writing for Intrigue: The Trifecta

Combine these three elements for maximum engagement:

### 1. Captivating Intro
- Hook that creates tension or curiosity
- Promises value worth the reader's time

### 2. Peak of Insight
- One "holy shit" moment
- The reason they'll remember your post
- Where you deliver your unique POV

### 3. Satisfying Ending
- Justifies why the piece was worth reading
- Ends on implications, not hype
- Gives them something to think about or do

---

## Hook Testing Checklist

Before you publish, ask:

✅ **Does my hook create curiosity?**
- Does it raise a question in the reader's mind?

✅ **Is there an information gap?**
- Have I revealed just enough to create tension?

✅ **Is it specific?**
- Does it use concrete details, not generalities?

✅ **Would I stop scrolling for this?**
- Be honest with yourself

✅ **Does it promise value?**
- Is it clear what they'll learn?

## Common Hook Mistakes

### ❌ Mistake #1: Too Generic
**Bad:** "Success requires hard work."
**Better:** "I worked 80-hour weeks for 2 years and almost went bankrupt. Here's what I'd do differently:"

### ❌ Mistake #2: No Stakes
**Bad:** "Here are some tips for social media."
**Better:** "If you're not doing these 3 things, you're invisible to the algorithm."

### ❌ Mistake #3: Giving Away Everything
**Bad:** "The secret is consistency. Post daily."
**Better:** "The secret isn't consistency. I'll explain:"

### ❌ Mistake #4: Asking Generic Questions
**Bad:** "What do you think about social media?"
**Better:** "Would you quit your job if your side project hit $10K MRR?"

---

## Quick Reference: Hook Checklist

Use this before publishing:

- ✅ Creates curiosity or tension
- ✅ Specific (numbers, names, details)
- ✅ Promises clear value
- ✅ Raises question without fully answering
- ✅ Would make me stop scrolling
- ✅ First 1-2 lines only (not buried)

---

## Putting It Together

**The formula:**
1. Start with a hook that creates tension/curiosity
2. Build the middle with frameworks, insights, or story
3. Sprinkle dopamine hits (surprising insights)
4. End with implications or clear takeaway

**Remember:**
- Hook buys you goodwill
- Peak moment makes it memorable
- Strong ending makes it valuable

**The goal:**
> Make people think: "I'm glad I read that."

---

**Master the hook, and you've won half the battle. The rest is just delivering on the promise you made.**
