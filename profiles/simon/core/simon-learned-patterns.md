# Learned Patterns & Refinements

This document captures real-world refinements from actual social posts. Use these insights to improve future posts.

---

## Pattern 1: News-Driven Posts

### Hook Structure for Breaking News

**Use "BREAKING:" prefix for immediate news**
```
BREAKING: [Company] just launched [feature/product]

[Immediate impact stat]
```

**Example:**
```
BREAKING: IBKR just launched 24/7 account funding with stablecoins (USDC, RLUSD, PYUSD coming). It's stock is up 5% since the announcement.
```

**Why it works:**
- Creates urgency
- Signals timeliness
- Grabs attention in feed

---

## Pattern 2: Problem-Framing Questions

### Use Explicit Questions to Frame Problems

**Direct question framing:**
```
What problem does this solve?

[The problem]
```

**Why it works:**
- Creates mental structure for readers
- Makes value proposition crystal clear
- More scannable than implied problems

**Example:**
```
What problem does this solve?

The "waiting for bank hours" tax.

You spot an opportunity Sunday night...
```

---

## Pattern 3: Spell Out Business Logic

### Don't Make Readers Infer - Connect the Dots Explicitly

**Pattern:**
```
[Observation about user behavior]

This means [specific outcome 1], [specific outcome 2], and [specific outcome 3]. Therefore [business result].
```

**Example:**
```
The share price tells the story: +5.56% on announcement day, +54.38% over the past year.

This means more people trading, for more hours, and more fees on the platform. Therefore more revenue.
```

**Why it works:**
- Removes cognitive load
- Shows you understand the business mechanics
- Builds credibility through clear reasoning

---

## Pattern 4: Clarifying Short Phrases

### Add Short Follow-Up Phrases to Clarify Jargon

**Pattern:**
```
[Technical term or concept]. [Plain English clarification].
```

**Example:**
```
this is alpha. An edge.
```

**Why it works:**
- Makes content accessible to wider audience
- Doesn't slow down expert readers (single sentence)
- Shows you care about clarity

---

## Pattern 5: Less Theatrical Scaffolding

### Cut Dramatic Framing That Feels Forced

**❌ Avoid:**
- "Here's what just died:"
- "This changes everything:"
- "The old way is over:"

**✅ Better:**
- Direct problem statement
- Use questions to frame
- Let the facts speak

**Example:**

**Before:**
```
Here's what just died:

The "waiting for bank hours" tax.
```

**After:**
```
What problem does this solve?

The "waiting for bank hours" tax.
```

## Pattern 6: Professional Competitive Framing

### Soften Aggressive Language for Professional Tone

**❌ Too aggressive:**
```
integrate stablecoins or watch customers leave for 24/7 liquidity
```

**✅ More professional:**
```
integrate stablecoins or watch customers choose a competitive product
```

**Why it matters:**
- Maintains authority without sounding combative
- More appropriate for executive/professional audience
- Still conveys competitive pressure

---

## Pattern 7: Cleaner Endings

### Cut Extra Dramatic Lines at the End

**❌ Too much:**
```
This isn't a feature. It's the new baseline.

Capital efficiency just got a permanent upgrade.
```

**✅ Cleaner:**
```
Stablecoins and 24/7 funding is the new baseline.
```

**Why it works:**
- End on the key point, not hype
- One strong line > three weaker lines
- Respects reader's intelligence

---

## Pattern 8: "BREAKING:" Prefix for Timeliness

### When to Use Breaking News Format

**Use when:**
- Announcement is <24 hours old
- Significant market impact (stock price movement, industry shift)
- You want to position yourself as first to analyze

**Structure:**
```
BREAKING: [Company/Event] [Action] [Key Detail]

[Immediate metric/impact]

[Rest of analysis]
```

**Timing:**
- Post within 4-12 hours of announcement
- Leverage the news cycle momentum

## Implementation Checklist

When writing social posts, check against these patterns:

### For News-Driven Posts:
- ✅ Consider "BREAKING:" prefix if <24hrs old
- ✅ Lead with the action, not the analysis
- ✅ Include immediate impact metric in hook

### For Clarity:
- ✅ Use explicit problem-framing questions
- ✅ Spell out business logic explicitly
- ✅ Add clarifying short phrases after jargon
- ✅ Check: "Would my non-expert friend understand this?"

### For Tone:
- ✅ Cut theatrical scaffolding
- ✅ Soften aggressive competitive language
- ✅ Clean ending - one strong line

### Before Publishing:
- ✅ Hook is news-forward if timely
- ✅ Business logic is explicit, not implied
- ✅ No jargon without clarification
- ✅ Professional tone maintained throughout
- ✅ Ending is clean and strong

## Real Example: Before & After

### Before (Initial Draft):
```
Interactive Brokers is trading at a 464% premium to fair value.

The market sees something analysts don't.

---

Here's what just died:

The "waiting for bank hours" tax.

[...]

[...]

Every other broker now faces a choice—integrate stablecoins or watch customers leave for 24/7 liquidity.
```

### After (Refined):
```
BREAKING: IBKR just launched 24/7 account funding with stablecoins (USDC, RLUSD, PYUSD coming). It's stock is up 5% since the announcement.

Near-instant processing. Weekends. Holidays. Always on.

That's a completely different game.

---

What problem does this solve?

The "waiting for bank hours" tax.

[...]

For IBKR's user base—sophisticated retail traders and institutions—this is alpha. An edge.

The share price tells the story: +5.56% on announcement day, +54.38% over the past year.

This means more people trading, for more hours, and more fees on the platform. Therefore more revenue.

---

Second-order effect:

Every other broker now faces a choice—integrate stablecoins or watch customers choose a competitive product.

Stablecoins and 24/7 funding is the new baseline.
```

**Key changes:**
1. ✅ Added "BREAKING:" prefix
2. ✅ Lead with the news, not valuation analysis
3. ✅ Used explicit problem-framing question
4. ✅ Spelled out business logic ("This means...")
5. ✅ Cut theatrical "Here's what just died"
6. ✅ Softened competitive language
7. ✅ Added clarifying phrase ("An edge.")
8. ✅ Cleaned up ending
