# Complete Post Creation Workflow

**Purpose:** End-to-end system from story discovery → hook selection → visual creation → publishing.

**Time estimate:** 20-30 minutes per post (once you have practice)

---

## Overview: The 6-Step Process

```
1. Story Discovery (5-10 min)
   ↓
2. Story Evaluation & Prioritization (2-3 min)
   ↓
3. Hook Selection & Drafting (5 min)
   ↓
4. Visual Strategy & Creation (5-10 min)
   ↓
5. Post Writing & Review (5 min)
   ↓
6. Publishing & Engagement (2 min + ongoing)
```

---

## Step 1: Story Discovery

**Time:** 5-10 minutes (morning routine)

**What you're doing:** Finding potential stories from your news sources

**Process:**

1. **Scan Tier 1 sources** (see `story-discovery-framework.md`)
   - TechCrunch FinTech section
   - The Block (crypto)
   - LinkedIn trending topics
   - Newsletter inbox (Linas, Stratechery, etc.)

2. **Capture interesting items**
   - Copy URL to Notion/doc
   - Quick note on why it caught your eye
   - Note dollar amounts, company names

3. **Look for patterns**
   - Is this the 2nd or 3rd example of something?
   - Does this validate a thesis you've had?
   - Is this a "finally" moment?

**Output:** List of 3-5 potential stories

**Tools:**
- Feedly (RSS aggregation)
- Notion/Airtable (story tracking)
- LinkedIn saved posts

---

## Step 2: Story Evaluation & Prioritization

**Time:** 2-3 minutes

**What you're doing:** Filtering stories through the 3-Filter System

**Process:**

### Apply The 3 Filters:

**Filter 1: Simon Filter** (Does this enable my voice?)
```
✅ Ask yourself:
- Can I genuinely say "I'm stumped" or ask real questions?
- Are there specific details (company structure, $, mechanics)?
- Can I do "Maybe X. Or Y." both-sides thinking?
- Is there infrastructure/rails angle?

Quick test: Can I write this in Simon's voice?
```

**Filter 2: Audience Filter** (Will they care?)
```
✅ Ask yourself:
- Would a FinTech PM send this to their team Slack?
- Are there second-order effects to analyze?
- Does this change how infrastructure works?
- Business model innovation or just feature?

Quick test: Would I forward this to a founder friend?
```

**Filter 3: Performance Filter** (Will it perform?)
```
✅ Check for:
- Specific dollar amounts? ($200M beats "a lot")
- Name-brand company? (Google, Stripe, etc.)
- Unexpected combo? (crypto + MrBeast)
- "Wait, what?" moment?
- Visual potential? (can you show it?)

Quick test: Would I stop scrolling for this?
```

### Assign Priority:

**Priority 1:** Passes all 3 filters → Publish today
**Priority 2:** Passes 2/3 filters → Develop this week
**Priority 3:** Interesting pattern → Watchlist
**Priority 4:** Fails 2+ filters → Skip

**Output:** 1-2 Priority 1 stories (or decision to skip posting today)

**Decision point:**
- If Priority 1 exists → Proceed to Step 3
- If no Priority 1 → Check Priority 2 backlog OR skip today
- Quality > consistency

## Step 3: Hook Selection & Drafting

**Time:** 5 minutes

**What you're doing:** Matching story to hook pattern and drafting opening

**Process:**

### 3A: Identify Story Type

**What's the core of this story?**

→ **Big company + $ amount?**
   Use Pattern 11 (Dollar Amount Specificity)

→ **Unexpected person move?**
   Use Pattern 8 (Person-Focused Narrative)

→ **Industry maturation signal?**
   Use Pattern 13 (The "Finally" Moment)

→ **Strategic company move?**
   Use Pattern 12 (Action Verbs with Stakes)

→ **Convergence of two worlds?**
   Use Pattern 9 (Stakes-First Framing)

→ **Genuinely surprising?**
   Use Pattern 10 (Exclamatory Emotion Hook)

**Reference:** `hooks-and-structure.md` for all patterns

---

### 3B: Draft Hook (First 210 Characters)

**Template based on pattern:**

**Pattern 8 (Person-Focused):**
```
This is [Name]. [Year/context].

[Surprising action/outcome].

But here's what's interesting: [analytical observation]
```

**Pattern 13 ("Finally"):**
```
[Topic] is finally [verb]: [Company] just [action],
and it's [unique angle]
```

**Pattern 9 (Stakes-First):**
```
[Company, ticker] just [invested/acquired] [$X] [into/from] [what].

Maybe this is about [bigger pattern].

Or it's [alternative interpretation].
```

---

### 3C: Simon Voice Check

**Before moving forward, verify:**

✅ **Specificity:**
- [ ] Company names (not "a company")
- [ ] Dollar amounts with M/B notation
- [ ] Tickers if public company
- [ ] Years, roles, specific context

✅ **Voice Authenticity:**
- [ ] Would Simon say this to a colleague?
- [ ] Preserves intellectual honesty?
- [ ] Genuine questions (not rhetorical)?
- [ ] "Maybe" used for actual speculation?

✅ **Performance:**
- [ ] Hook in first 210 characters?
- [ ] "Wait, what?" moment comes first?
- [ ] Specific enough to be interesting?

**If all checks pass → Proceed to Step 4**
**If voice feels off → Revise or pick different story**

**Output:** Hook drafted, voice-checked, ready for visual

---

## Step 4: Visual Strategy & Creation

**Time:** 5-10 minutes

**What you're doing:** Matching hook to visual format and sourcing/creating image

**Process:**

### 4A: Select Visual Format

**Use the Decision Tree from `visual-strategy.md`:**

**Person-Focused Hook (Pattern 8)?**
→ Quote Over Face OR Person + Context

**"Finally" Moment (Pattern 13)?**
→ Before/After Comparison OR Timeline

**Dollar Amount (Pattern 11)?**
→ Logos + Number OR Data Viz

**Stakes-First (Pattern 9)?**
→ Venn Diagram OR Side-by-Side Logos

**Action Verbs (Pattern 12)?**
→ Flow Diagram OR Strategic Map

**Multiple Examples?**
→ Comparison Grid OR Timeline

---

### 4B: Source Base Images

**For people:**
```
1. Google: "[Name] headshot" or "[Name] linkedin"
2. LinkedIn profile (download photo)
3. Company press kit
4. Crunchbase
```

**For logos:**
```
1. Clearbit API: https://logo.clearbit.com/[domain].com
2. Press kit: [company].com/press
3. Crunchbase
4. Wikipedia
```

**For screenshots:**
```
1. Take your own (visit product)
2. Official product pages
3. YouTube demos (screenshot)
4. Company blog posts
```

**Verification:**
- ✅ Press kits (for media use)
- ✅ LinkedIn photos (fair use)
- ✅ Self-created screenshots
- ✅ Logos (nominative fair use)
- ❌ Watermarked stock photos
- ❌ Copyrighted without license

---

### 4C: Create Visual

**Tools:**
- **Canva** (templates, quick)
- **Figma** (professional, flexible)
- **Keynote/PPT** (surprisingly good)

**Specs:**
```
Dimensions: 1200x628px (landscape) or 1200x1200px (square)
Format: JPG or PNG
Size: <5MB
Text: Readable on mobile (test at 25% size)
```

**Quality checks:**
- [ ] Mobile readable?
- [ ] High contrast?
- [ ] Professional (not marketing-y)?
- [ ] Legally safe?
- [ ] Enhances hook (not random)?

**Output:** Image file ready to upload

## Step 5: Post Writing & Review

**Time:** 5 minutes

**What you're doing:** Writing full post body and final review

**Process:**

### 5A: Post Structure

**Standard LinkedIn post structure:**

```
[Hook - first 210 characters]
↓
[Value delivery - analysis, context, insights]
↓
[Payoff - takeaway, implication, or CTA]
```

**Example:**

```
Crypto is finally investing in creators: [HOOK]
BitMine (BMNR), world's largest corporate ETH holder,
just put $200M into MrBeast's company.

Not tokens. Not NFTs. Equity. [END HOOK - 210 chars]

---

This is interesting for a few reasons: [VALUE DELIVERY START]

First, BitMine Immersion Technologies (ticker: BMNR) is a public company
set up specifically to acquire and hold ETH. They're sitting on the
largest corporate Ethereum position in the world.

Second, MrBeast isn't taking crypto native investment tools—
no tokens, no community DAOs, no NFT drops. Just traditional equity.

Third, this might signal crypto-native capital finally growing up.
Crypto has been trying to "do" creators forever. Maybe the answer
all along was to just... invest in them. [VALUE DELIVERY END]

---

Watch for more crypto-native funds investing in creator IP. [PAYOFF]
Or it's the most 2026 thing I'll see all year. [ALTERNATIVE]
```

**Key elements:**
- ✅ Hook in first 210 chars (before "see more")
- ✅ Line breaks for scannability
- ✅ Specifics (company structure, ticker, details)
- ✅ Simon's voice (both-sides, genuine questions)
- ✅ Clear takeaway or implication

---

### 5B: Final Review Checklist

**Voice:**
- [ ] Sounds like Simon (both-sides thinking, humble speculation)?
- [ ] Genuine questions vs rhetorical?
- [ ] Avoids generic excitement ("OMG!", "INSANE!")?
- [ ] 0-1 emoji max?

**Structure:**
- [ ] Hook in first 210 characters?
- [ ] Line breaks for scannability?
- [ ] Logical flow (hook → value → payoff)?
- [ ] Clear ending (not trailing off)?

**Specificity:**
- [ ] Company names + tickers?
- [ ] Dollar amounts with M/B?
- [ ] Years, dates, context?
- [ ] No vague claims?

**Performance:**
- [ ] Visual attached and optimized?
- [ ] "Wait, what?" moment clear?
- [ ] Worth stopping scroll?
- [ ] Invites engagement (questions, discussion)?

**If all checks pass → Ready to publish**

**Output:** Final post text + image ready for LinkedIn

## Step 6: Publishing & Engagement

**Time:** 2 minutes to publish + ongoing engagement

**Process:**

### 6A: Optimal Publishing Time

**Based on LinkedIn algorithm research:**
- **Best times:** Weekdays, 8 AM - 2 PM (audience's local time)
- **Optimal cadence:** 2-5 posts per week
- **Golden hour:** First 60-90 minutes after publishing

**For Simon's audience (FinTech professionals):**
- Tuesday-Thursday preferred
- Morning (8-10 AM) or lunch (12-1 PM)
- Avoid: Late Friday, weekends (unless major breaking news)

---

### 6B: Publishing Checklist

**On LinkedIn:**
1. [ ] Copy post text
2. [ ] Upload image/video
3. [ ] Preview on mobile (does it look good?)
4. [ ] Double-check first 210 chars visible
5. [ ] Proofread one final time
6. [ ] Click "Post"

**Immediately after posting:**
1. [ ] Pin to profile (if important)
2. [ ] Share to Twitter/X (if cross-posting)
3. [ ] Set 15-min timer to check back

---

### 6C: Engagement Strategy (First 90 Minutes)

**Why it matters:**
- First 60-90 minutes = "golden hour"
- Algorithm looks for engagement velocity
- Early momentum = beyond-follower distribution

**Your job in first 90 minutes:**

**0-15 minutes:**
- Monitor for immediate comments
- Respond to first 3-5 comments quickly
- Like all genuine comments
- Fix any typos (edit if needed)

**15-60 minutes:**
- Check every 15 minutes
- Reply to thoughtful comments
- Ask follow-up questions
- Encourage discussion

**60-90 minutes:**
- Final check
- Respond to remaining comments
- Note engagement rate (compared to baseline)

**After 90 minutes:**
- Check 2-3x per day
- Respond to new comments within 24h
- Don't let good discussions die

---

### 6D: Comment Response Guidelines

**When someone comments:**

**✅ DO:**
- Reply to genuine questions
- Acknowledge different perspectives
- Ask follow-up questions
- Say "I don't know" when you don't
- Use their name (personalize)
- Keep responses conversational

**❌ DON'T:**
- Generic "Thanks!" on every comment
- Defend your take defensively
- Ignore thoughtful critiques
- Let trolls bait you into arguments
- Copy-paste same response

**Example responses:**

**Thoughtful agreement:**
```
"Exactly! And I'm curious if this pattern extends to [related area].
Have you seen examples of that?"
```

**Thoughtful disagreement:**
```
"That's a fair point. I hadn't thought about [their perspective].
Maybe the answer is somewhere between our takes?"
```

**Genuine question:**
```
"Great question. Honestly, I'm not sure. My guess would be [speculation],
but I could be wrong. Anyone else have insights?"
```

**Generic praise:**
```
[Like the comment]
OR
"Appreciate it! 🙏"
[Keep it brief, move to substantive comments]
```

## Performance Tracking

**After each post, note:**

**Quantitative:**
- Impressions (reach)
- Engagement rate (reactions + comments per 1000 followers)
- Comments (quantity and quality)
- Reposts/shares
- Profile visits
- Click-throughs (if link included)

**Qualitative:**
- Comment quality (discussion vs "great post!")
- Types of people engaging (target audience?)
- New connections/followers (right people?)
- DMs or business inquiries

**Pattern recognition:**
- Which hook patterns perform best?
- Which visual formats get most engagement?
- Which topics resonate most?
- What time/day is optimal?

## Common Mistakes & Solutions

### Mistake 1: Posting Without Visual
**Problem:** Text-only post
**Impact:** 0.6x engagement (algorithm penalty)
**Solution:** No visual ready? Don't post yet. Wait until you have it.

### Mistake 2: Forcing Posts on Weak Stories
**Problem:** "I should post today" → posts Priority 3 story
**Impact:** Trains audience to expect low quality
**Solution:** Quality > consistency. Skip if no Priority 1 story.

### Mistake 3: Publishing and Ghosting
**Problem:** Post and don't check for 8 hours
**Impact:** Miss golden hour, algorithm penalizes
**Solution:** Block 90 minutes after posting to monitor/engage

### Mistake 4: Generic Responses
**Problem:** "Thanks!" on every comment
**Impact:** Looks automated, kills discussion
**Solution:** Thoughtful responses OR just like comment

### Mistake 5: No Pattern Tracking
**Problem:** Can't tell what's working
**Impact:** Keep repeating low performers
**Solution:** Track engagement in simple spreadsheet

## Emergency: Breaking News Protocol

**When major news breaks and you want to post FAST:**

### Speed Run (15 minutes total):

**Step 1:** Verify story (2 min)
- Check primary source
- Confirm dollar amounts, names
- Ensure it's real (not rumor)

**Step 2:** Quick filter (1 min)
- Simon Filter: Can I add unique angle?
- Performance: Is it viral-worthy?
- Skip Audience Filter (assume yes if breaking)

**Step 3:** Speed hook (3 min)
- Use Pattern 10 (Emotion) or Pattern 13 (Finally)
- Front-load the surprise
- Get specific quickly

**Step 4:** Simple visual (5 min)
- Logos + dollar amount (quickest)
- OR screenshot from source
- Don't overthink - speed matters

**Step 5:** Brief post (2 min)
- Hook + 2-3 paragraphs
- Simon's take (genuine question okay)
- Publish immediately

**Step 6:** Engage hard (2 min + ongoing)
- You're early - discussion will form
- Be present in comments
- Become the discussion hub

**Trade-off:** Speed over polish
**When to use:** Major industry news <3h old
**When to skip:** If 100 people already posted, you're late
