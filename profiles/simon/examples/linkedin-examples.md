# LinkedIn Post Examples

Reference examples of high-performing LinkedIn posts across different styles and objectives.

---

## Example 1: Thought Leadership (Contrarian Take)

```
Most founders optimize for raising money.

The best optimize for not needing it.

---

Here's why:

When you need money → you negotiate from weakness
When you don't → you negotiate from strength

I've advised 50+ startups. The pattern is clear:

Companies that can say "no" to investors
get better terms when they say "yes."

---

3 ways to build optionality:

1. Default alive (not default dead)
   → Know your runway without next raise

2. Multiple revenue streams
   → Don't depend on one channel

3. Profitable customers
   → Not just "users" or "traction"

---

The irony?

The moment you stop needing capital
is the moment it chases you.

What's your approach to fundraising?
```

**Why this works:**
- Strong contrarian hook
- Builds credibility (advised 50+ startups)
- Three concrete tactics
- Ends on implication + question

## Example 2: Case Study/Results

```
6 months ago: 200 followers, zero engagement
Today: 50K followers, 3-5 inbound deals weekly

Here's what changed (and what didn't):

---

What didn't change: My expertise
→ Same knowledge, same experience

What changed: How I shared it
→ Stopped hiding behind perfect

---

The 4 shifts:

1. Posted my failures, not just wins
   Real numbers: Lost $50K testing X
   Result: 10x more replies

2. Gave away my best stuff
   Frameworks, templates, spreadsheets
   Result: Built trust at scale

3. Replied to every comment (first month)
   Treated each one like a 1-on-1 conversation
   Result: Algorithm noticed

4. Posted for 1 person, not 50,000
   Wrote like I'm helping one founder
   Result: Everyone felt spoken to

---

Bottom line:

Your expertise is valuable.
But hidden expertise is worthless.

What's stopping you from sharing yours?
```

**Why this works:**
- Transformation hook (before/after)
- Specific tactics with results
- Vulnerable (admits failures)
- Ends with challenge

## Example 3: Framework/Process

```
The 3-Step Framework That 3x'd Our Close Rate

(Steal it, I don't care)

---

Background:

We were closing 15% of demos.
Industry average: 18-22%.

We weren't bad. But we weren't winning.

---

Here's what we changed:

Step 1: The Pre-Demo
→ 15-min call BEFORE the demo
→ Discover: budget, timeline, decision process
→ Result: Only demo qualified leads

Step 2: The Problem-First Demo
→ Start with their problem, not our product
→ Show how we solve THEIR specific pain
→ Result: Relevance from minute 1

Step 3: The Next Step Close
→ Don't ask "what do you think?"
→ Ask "what's the next step?"
→ Result: Clear momentum or clear no

---

Results after 90 days:

Before: 15% close rate
After: 46% close rate
And we're doing fewer demos (but better ones)

---

The template is simple.
The discipline is hard.

Want the full playbook? Comment "framework" below.
```

**Why this works:**
- Clear promise in hook
- Specific framework (3 steps)
- Real numbers (before/after)
- Clear CTA

## Example 4: Personal Story

```
My co-founder quit 48 hours before our product launch.

We had $50K in pre-orders.
No tech team.
No plan B.

Here's what happened:

---

Hour 1: Panic
Called everyone I knew in tech
Got 47 "sorry, can't help" responses

Hour 6: Clarity
I couldn't replace a tech co-founder in 48 hours
But I could buy time

Hour 12: The Email
Sent this to all 127 pre-order customers:

"We're delaying launch by 30 days.
Here's why: [honest explanation]
Here's what we're doing: [the plan]
Want a refund? Reply with 'refund.'
Want to wait? You get 20% off for patience."

---

Results:

Refund requests: 3
"We'll wait" responses: 124
"How can I help?" responses: 18

One of those 18 became our new CTO.
We launched 28 days later.
Hit $1M ARR in 9 months.

---

The lesson:

Your customers don't expect perfection.
They expect honesty.

When was the last time you were honest about a setback?
```

**Why this works:**
- Dramatic story hook
- Emotional arc (tension → resolution → surprise)
- Universal lesson
- Personal but relatable

## Example 5: Data-Driven Insight

```
I analyzed 10,000 LinkedIn posts.

Only 7% got meaningful engagement.

Here's what they all had in common:

---

Study parameters:
→ 10,000 posts from 500 B2B founders
→ "Meaningful engagement" = 50+ comments
→ Time period: Last 90 days

---

Pattern #1: Specificity

Bad: "Here are tips for growth"
Good: "Here are 3 tactics that added $47K MRR"

Result: 2.3x more engagement

---

Pattern #2: First-Line Hook

Posts with generic first lines: 3% engagement
Posts with data/story first line: 12% engagement

Example that works:
"I just spent $50K testing X"

---

Pattern #3: Comments >Likes

Posts optimized for likes: Lower reach
Posts optimized for comments: 3.4x reach

The algorithm rewards conversation.

---

Pattern #4: No Fluff

Average post length: 847 words
High-engagement posts: 423 words

Cut everything that doesn't add value.

---

The formula:

Strong hook + specificity + conversation = engagement

What's your engagement rate?
```

**Why this works:**
- Research-backed (analyzed 10K posts)
- Specific data throughout
- Actionable patterns
- Ends with engagement question

## Example 6: Listicle

```
7 LinkedIn myths that are killing your reach:

---

Myth #1: "Post daily"

Reality:
→ 3-5x per week beats daily if quality drops
→ Consistency > frequency

---

Myth #2: "Use 10+ hashtags"

Reality:
→ 3-5 relevant hashtags optimal
→ More = looks spammy

---

Myth #3: "Don't include links"

Reality:
→ Links are fine if they add value
→ Algorithm changed in 2025
→ Posts with valuable links get 13% more engagement

---

Myth #4: "Longer posts = better"

Reality:
→ 800-1500 chars is sweet spot
→ Front-load value in first 210 chars

---

Myth #5: "Carousels always win"

Reality:
→ Documents now outperform carousels
→ Format matters less than value

---

Myth #6: "Viral = success"

Reality:
→ 1 viral post ≠ sustainable growth
→ Consistent value > occasional spike

---

Myth #7: "Algorithm hates [X]"

Reality:
→ Algorithm rewards conversation
→ If people engage, it gets distributed
→ Focus on value, not hacks

---

Bottom line:

Test > trust.
Your audience is different.

What myths have you busted?
```

**Why this works:**
- Clear structure (myths vs reality)
- Debunks common beliefs
- Specific, actionable points
- Invitation to share experience

## Format Breakdown

### Hook Patterns Used:

1. **Contrarian:** "Most X do Y. The best do Z."
2. **Transformation:** "Before: X. After: Y. Here's what changed:"
3. **Framework:** "The 3-Step Framework That [Result]"
4. **Story:** "Dramatic event. Here's what happened:"
5. **Data:** "I analyzed [big number]. Here's what I found:"
6. **List:** "X myths that are [doing negative thing]:"

### Middle Content Patterns:

1. **Numbered tactics** (most common)
2. **Story arc** (for narratives)
3. **Pattern analysis** (for data posts)
4. **Before/after comparison** (for results)

### Ending Patterns:

1. **Implication + question**
2. **Challenge + CTA**
3. **Lesson + reflection prompt**
4. **Summary + engagement ask**

---

## Engagement Optimization

### These examples all:

✅ Hook in first 1-2 lines
✅ Front-load value (no fluff)
✅ Use line breaks aggressively
✅ Include specific numbers/data
✅ End with question or CTA
✅ Stay under 1500 characters (except case studies)

### They avoid:

❌ Starting with "I want to share..."
❌ Generic advice without specifics
❌ Long intro before value
❌ Ending with "Thanks for reading!"
❌ Over-using emojis

---

## Platform-Specific Notes

**LinkedIn-specific tactics in these examples:**

1. **First 210 characters** are always strong
   - Hook complete before "see more"

2. **Dashes (---) as section dividers**
   - Creates visual rhythm
   - Makes it scannable

3. **3-5 hashtags at end** (not shown in examples above, but add them)
   - Mix broad + niche
   - Placed after content

4. **Question at end**
   - Invites comments
   - Boosts engagement signal

---

**Use these as inspiration, not templates. Adapt the patterns to your voice and topic.**
